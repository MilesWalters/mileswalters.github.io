Carnegie Science · Research Intern

Looking for signs of life in chemical data

Earth & Planets Laboratory · Summers 2025–2026

At Carnegie Science, I write code in Python and R to clean and align chemical data from biological and nonbiological samples, compare their patterns, and train models to tell them apart. The goal is to find signs of life by looking at the chemical pattern of a sample as a whole, even if it does not contain a molecule we already associate with life.

My work has contributed to several conference presentations, and I’m now helping write a paper about what we’ve found.

Machine learning · astrobiology · data analysis

Independent research · 2026

Detecting AI-generated writing

I built and trained an AI detector that looks at how people put sentences together, including parts of speech and verb tense. I wanted to understand which patterns could help distinguish human writing from AI-generated text, while also being careful about incorrectly flagging someone’s own work.

About

I like figuring out how things work.

That started with taking apart toys as a kid, and it’s a big part of why I study artificial intelligence now.

When I was younger, I would take apart my toys because I wanted to know how they worked. I still approach technology in much the same way, trying things and asking questions until I understand what is happening and why.

I started coding in Python and became interested in machine learning because of how it could be used to solve problems in other fields. Since then, I’ve worked on projects in science, language, health, and education, and each one has given me a different way to think about what AI can do.

I want to understand the models I build well enough to explain how they work, recognize when they’re wrong, and think carefully about how people use them.
